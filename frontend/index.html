Generative AI Candidates

Our Conditional VAE generates novel siRNA modification patterns targeted at 85% knockdown. These patterns have never been published.

How does an AI generate a drug candidate?

We trained a neural network on 3,700+ real siRNA experiments. The network learns the “language” of modification chemistry — which combinations tend to produce high knockdown. Then we ask it: “Generate a new pattern that should achieve 85% knockdown.”

The network outputs novel candidates that sit in the unexplored regions of the modification space. This is the same class of approach used by Insilico Medicine’s Chemistry42 for small molecule drug design.

Low = similar to known patterns. High = more adventurous.

What We Cannot Do

We are transparent about what OligoVoid cannot do:

  • No wet-lab validation: all results are computational
  • Limited chemical diversity: most training data uses 2’-OMe and 2’-F; other modifications have fewer examples
  • Context-free: we don’t model the target mRNA sequence (OligoFormer does this better — use it for sequence design)
  • GP uncertainty is approximate: not a rigorous Bayesian guarantee

OligoVoid is best used as: a gap-finding and experiment-prioritization tool. It answers “what should I try next?” not “will this definitely work?”
