What is siRNA?

  Normal gene expression:
    DNA  →  mRNA  →  Protein

  With siRNA therapy:
    DNA  →  mRNA  ←  siRNA
                 |
            [SILENCED]

Small interfering RNA (siRNA) is a tiny molecule that silences disease-causing genes. It intercepts the messenger RNA before it can make harmful proteins. Eight FDA-approved drugs already use this mechanism to treat conditions from high cholesterol to rare genetic diseases.

Why chemical modifications?

Naked RNA survives about 15 minutes in your bloodstream. Chemical modifications act as protective armor: they shield each position on the strand from enzymes that would destroy it, while preserving its ability to silence the target gene.

There are 8 major modification types (2'-OMe, 2'-F, LNA, cEt, DNA, RNA, UNA, MOE) that can be applied to each of 42 positions on the siRNA duplex.

The design space

8 modifications across 42 positions means 842 possible combinations — more than 1037 patterns. That is more than the number of atoms in a human body. Even if every lab on Earth tested one pattern per second, it would take longer than the age of the universe to explore them all.

Only about 3,700 patterns have ever been published. That leaves 92% completely uncharted.

What is a “void”?

A void is a modification pattern that has never been published or tested. Like dark matter in the universe — it should exist, but nobody has looked there yet. About 92% of the modification space is voids. Some of those voids could be the next breakthrough drug.

What can YOU do here?

OligoVoid maps every void, scores them with biophysics rules and machine learning, then ranks which untested combinations are most likely to work. You can:

  • Explore the dark matter map of siRNA chemistry
  • See which untested patterns score highest
  • Validate predictions against FDA-approved drugs
  • Build and score your own custom patterns
  • Generate entirely novel candidates with AI

This is the 21-position pattern builder. Click any position to change its modification. Each of the 8 modification types has different properties:

2'-OMe — Gold standard. Good stability, good RISC tolerance. The workhorse.
2'-F — Mimics RNA geometry. Best RISC loading. Slightly less stable than OMe.
LNA — Locks sugar in C3'-endo. Extreme stability but stiff. Hurts RISC if in seed.
cEt — LNA sibling. Similar stability, slightly better tolerance in some positions.
DNA — Destabilizing. Used strategically to lower Tm at specific positions.
RNA — Unmodified. Natural but vulnerable to nucleases. Baseline reference.
UNA — Unlocked nucleic acid. Very flexible. Good at position 1 for thermodynamic asymmetry.
MOE — 2'-O-methoxyethyl. Great nuclease resistance but bulky. Avoid in seed region.

How OligoVoid Works — The Full Story

7 chapters, from raw data to recommendations. Written so anyone can understand.

1

Collect Real Data

I gathered 3,723 real siRNA experiments from 9 published studies (Huesken 2005, Takahashi 2009, and others). Each experiment tells us: “this siRNA sequence, with these chemical modifications, achieved X% gene knockdown.”

Think of it like: collecting 3,723 restaurant reviews. Each review says what ingredients were used and how good the dish was. Now you can learn which ingredients tend to make great food.
2

Map the Known Universe

For each of 42 positions on the siRNA (21 guide, 21 passenger), I count how many times each of 8 modifications has been tested. This creates a 42×8 heatmap. Red = voids (never tested). Green = well-studied.

Result: About 92% of the space has never been published. That is the “dark matter.”
3

Score Every Candidate (3 Layers)

Each untested combination gets scored by three independent systems:

Layer 1
Biophysics Rules — Does this pattern survive in blood? Can the cell's machinery use it? Will it hit the right target? Based on published chemistry rules. (4 sub-scores)
Layer 2
Gaussian Process (GP) — A machine learning model trained on 3,723 real experiments. It predicts efficacy AND tells you how confident it is.
Layer 3
CVAE Novelty — A generative AI that learned the “language” of siRNA chemistry. It checks: is this pattern truly novel, or a trivial variation?
4

Generate Novel Patterns

The CVAE can also generate entirely new modification patterns. I tell it: “create something that should achieve 85% knockdown.” It generates patterns in the unexplored regions of chemical space.

Quality check: 96.5% validity, 100% novelty (never seen in training data), and high diversity (not just copies of each other).
5

Prioritize with VPA

Void-Prioritized Acquisition (VPA) is the novel contribution. Standard active learning uses Expected Improvement (EI) — “pick the pattern most likely to be the best.” But EI keeps testing similar patterns. VPA adds a bonus for novelty: VPA(x) = EI(x) × (1 + α × novelty).

Analogy: Imagine you are exploring a cave. EI keeps going deeper into tunnels you have already found. VPA says “that unexplored tunnel over there might connect to something big — let us check it first.”
6

Validate Against Real Drugs

I test OligoVoid's predictions against 5 FDA-approved siRNA drugs that were NOT in the training data. This is the honest, out-of-sample validation. If the tool cannot correctly identify known drugs as high-efficacy, it has no business recommending untested ones.

Transparency: I show the full results — including where the model gets it wrong — on the FDA Reality Check tab. No cherry-picking.
7

What OligoVoid Cannot Do

I am transparent about the limitations:

  • No wet-lab validation: all results are computational
  • Limited chemical diversity: most training data uses 2'-OMe and 2'-F; other modifications have fewer examples
  • Context-free: OligoVoid does not model the target mRNA sequence (OligoFormer does this better — use it for sequence design)
  • GP uncertainty is approximate: not a rigorous Bayesian guarantee
Bottom line: OligoVoid is best used as a gap-finding and experiment-prioritization tool. It answers “what should I try next?” not “will this definitely work?”
