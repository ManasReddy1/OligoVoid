Founder OS

Not built yet

A search engine for consumer app ideas that do not exist yet. It maps the design space, marks what already ships, finds the regions a recent capability change just made viable, and then tries very hard to kill everything it finds.

Correction to the premise

“88” is hours, not years.

The 10,000-agent run was OpenAI’s Navier–Stokes effort, 1–5 September 2026, about 88 hours. The problem dates to 1934 and has been a Millennium Prize problem since 2000. Four things about it matter more than the agent count:

  • It answered the weaker question. The official statement has four propositions; two permit a smooth external forcing term. That is the one they proved. OpenAI wrote: “We do not intend to claim the Millennium Prize for this result.”
  • The mathematical idea was not the model’s. The method is the Córdoba–Martínez-Zoroa cascade, circa 2021–2023, no AI involved.
  • Humans with model assistance got there first. Two mathematicians had the same class of result formally verified on 22 August, before OpenAI’s internal model finished training on 28 August.
  • No ablation shows 10,000 agents was necessary. The only comparison they disclosed is that an easier result in the same effort needed roughly 100 agents.

What is worth copying is the machinery underneath: a sound verifier, a generator separated from it, decomposition by hypothesis, an archive that maintains diversity, structured shared memory, and a staged evaluation cascade.

Agent count is a dependent variable of oracle quality

Startup ideas have no proof checker. A consumer app idea is the canonical reverse-asymmetric artifact: generating a thousand costs dollars, verifying one costs a market experiment. So the central engineering problem of this system is manufacturing the best available proxy oracle, and being explicit about where it leaks.
Control flow

One writer, five stages, no agent-to-agent calls

Three independent teams converged on the same rule: parallel agents are safe for reading and exploring, dangerous for writing and synthesising. So the orchestrator owns all state and every other agent is a stateless reader.

Representation

An idea is a typed slot vector

The same structure this repository already uses for siRNA modification patterns. Slots one to four form the archive coordinate. Every slot records where it came from: harvested evidence, an inherited parent, or the model’s own invention.

Void detection

Occupied, tombstoned, or void

Shipped products project into the same space. A cell with a live product is occupied. A cell where everything died is tombstoned, and it is the highest-value region when paired with a new unlock, because “right idea, wrong decade” is the most reliable source of opportunity. A cell with neither, next to a capability that just got cheap, is the frontier.

Illustrative. The axes and unlocks are real and sourced; the cell states are hand-set to show how the encoding works, not pipeline output. The real grid is four dimensions deep and holds one elite per cell, which is what makes “we already have an idea like that” a structural fact rather than a matter of opinion.

Panel capped at seven, on clean context, sharded into isolated subgroups, drawn from different model families where available, with all seniority language stripped. Each of those five constraints exists because of a measured result. Any single unanswerable attack is a hard kill the judge cannot overrule.

Scoring

Two heads, joined harmonically, never averaged

Blind reviewers rated model-generated ideas more novel than expert ideas. Then 43 experts spent a hundred hours each actually executing them, and the model ideas’ scores dropped further than the human ideas’ on every dimension, in places flipping the ranking. “Novel-sounding” is cheap and models are superhuman at it. “Actually good” is not measurable at ideation time. Collapsing both into one number is exactly where that gap hides.

# head 1 — is this actually new? novelty = harmonic_mean(originality, quality) originality = min(distance_to_cold_model_priors, distance_to_shipped_products) # head 2 — could you actually ship and sell it? viability = harmonic_mean(buildability, unit_economics, distribution_loop, timing) # reported side by side, always. ranked pairwise within each head. # the composite only selects what reaches a real-world probe: shortlist = 100 * harmonic_mean(novelty, viability) * survival_multiplier

Harmonic throughout, because the harmonic mean is chosen specifically to punish either term being low. A weighted sum lets a fatal weakness hide behind strong unrelated dimensions: an idea that is brilliant and unbuildable would land in the middle instead of at zero. Then each survivor is turned into three to five falsifiable binary forecasts with resolution dates, and the system tracks its own Brier score over time — the one thing no rubric can give you, a scoring function that is eventually scored itself.

The honest ceiling: published benchmarks on venture-outcome prediction reach about a 4.7× lift over a 0.78% base rate, with frontier models beating human baselines. That is roughly a 4% hit rate. The deliverable is a ranked shortlist with kill conditions and a cheap decisive test. Never a verdict.
Deliberate omissions

What this design refuses to do, and why

Each of these is the intuitive way to build the system, and each is ruled out by something measured rather than by taste.

TemptingWhy not
A 10,000-agent committeeCoordination cost is super-linear, returns go negative past 45% single-agent accuracy, error amplification hits 17.2×, and no ablation supports the headline
“Think like a genius” personasMeasured: visionary-founder personas produced 50.40 unique idea categories against 56.97 for ordinary personas. The celebrity voice is worse than a random normal person
Agents arguing to refine ideasAcross 36 configurations, none of five debate methods beat plain chain-of-thought. Generate in isolation first, then attack
Asking the model to be novelPrompting for novelty and raising temperature buy originality by spending quality, and barely move the frontier. Temperature’s effect is U-shaped
A panel of personas as a juryJuries help only when errors are uncorrelated. Five personas of one model is one judge wearing hats
Rating ideas one to tenPairwise comparison agrees with human preference more reliably, and the gap widens precisely on subjective judgments
Letting the system grade itselfThe closest existing system reports progress on a curve that is largely self-rated. Rising internal scores with no external check means the ranker has learned to like the generator
Enterprise, regulated, hardware, or over-budget ideasRejected deterministically at gate zero, not left to an agent’s judgment

Build order

Eight phases, and the first three carry the weight

Phase 1Skeleton. Genome schema, gate-zero validity rules, world-model store, orchestrator with dual ledger and stall counter, cost governor
Phase 2Evidence. Three harvesters plus normalisation into typed primitives, citation enforced at write time
Phase 3Void map. Occupancy grid, void classification, originality distances, the obviousness gate
Phase 4Genesis: archive, island theses, six operators, the generation envelope, diversity monitor
Phase 5Oracle ladder: grounding, buildability, adversarial panel, sneaky advocate, tournament
Phase 6Calibration against labelled historical launches, golden set, Brier tracking
Phase 7Founder Console: morning brief, dossiers, kill log, probe queue
Phase 8Probe loop and active-learning feedback — closing the loop to reality

A system with phases one to three and a single naive generator will beat a system with a magnificent agent roster and no evidence layer. That is not a guess; it is what the ablation measured.

Before the build starts

Five things only you can decide

Everything else in this document is settled by evidence. These five are judgment calls about your money, your time, and your appetite.

01

How many model families?

Heterogeneous critics are the only debate intervention that reliably helps, and juries only work when their errors are uncorrelated. That means paying for a second and third provider’s API alongside the first.

Recommended: two families minimum for the panel and the judge. One family works, but the panel gets logged as a single judge with variance rather than independent votes.

02

What is the monthly running budget?

This sets cycle size directly. Batch processing plus prompt caching stacks to roughly 95% off input, and cost scales with survivors rather than with how much you generate.

Recommended: start at $50–$150 a month for a nightly cycle, and skip the model router entirely until spend passes about $200, below which it costs more engineering time than it saves.

03

Will you actually run the probes?

Gate seven is the only contact with ground truth, and it needs real money and a human decision. Without it, rising internal scores mean only that the ranker has learned to like the generator.

Recommended: commit $200 a month for probes, or cut the probe layer and accept that the system is a ranked hypothesis generator rather than a validator.

04

Do you want the calibration backtest?

It is what separates a measured scoring function from a confident one, and the labelled historical data is public. It is also a weekend of work that produces no new ideas.

Recommended: yes, and run it before trusting a single ranking. It is the cheapest credibility available.

05

Where does this live?

Right now it sits beside OligoVoid, which is genuinely apt — it reuses the same void-detection machinery — but the two projects have nothing else in common.

Recommended: keep it here through phase three while the shared abstraction is doing real work, then split it into its own repository.
